To solve the compound inequality 

$$0 < 2x - 1 	ext{ and } 2x - 1 < 2x + 1,$$

we need to solve each part separately and then find the intersection of the solutions.

### Solving the first inequality:

$$0 < 2x - 1.$$

Add 1 to both sides:

$$1 < 2x.$$

Divide both sides by 2:

$$rac{1}{2} < x.$$

### Solving the second inequality:

$$2x - 1 < 2x + 1.$$

Subtract $2x$ from both sides:

$$-1 < 1.$$

This inequality is always true, so it doesn't impose any additional restrictions on $x$. 

### Combining the solutions:

The solution to the compound inequality is simply the solution to the first inequality, since the second inequality is always true:

$$x > rac{1}{2}.$$

$$(rac{1}{2}, 	ext{∞}).$$
